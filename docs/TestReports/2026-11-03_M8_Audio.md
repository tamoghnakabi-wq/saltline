# Test report: game audio, 2026-11-03

All results below come from runs on 2026-11-03 on one Apple Silicon Mac (M4), using the macOS release build (0.1.0,
protocol 4). Nothing here is estimated. Loudness figures are BS.1770 K-weighted LUFS computed by scripts in this
session from the generated files and from mixes the game recorded. Nobody listened to the game for this report: the
checks are measurements, play counts and recordings.

**Not tested yet:**
- Listening on real speakers or headphones. The laptop figures come from a speaker model (a 200 Hz high-pass), not
  from a MacBook.
- Windows.
- Kappa in a live match with gunfire. The combat tests run on the Arena test map. Kappa's ambience and machinery
  emitters were checked by an EditMode test and in the UI test's capture.
- Eight players. The busiest run used 15 of the 52 voices.

## 1. What was built

`docs/AUDIO.md` describes the system; in short:

- **Sounds.** `ArtSource/Audio/gen_sfx.py` synthesises 56 sounds (98 files with variants, about 10 MB of WAV),
  covering gunshots near and far, weapon handling, footsteps per surface, impacts per surface, player sounds, hit
  feedback, interface sounds, match stings and ambience. Nothing is sampled or downloaded.
- **Bank.** `AudioFactory` imports them (mono Vorbis) and sets each sound's volume from its measured loudness and a
  target for its kind.
- **Playback.** `GameAudio` plays everything:
  - from 52 pooled voices;
  - with distance culling, speed-of-sound delay past 25 m, a near/far crossfade for gunshots, and occlusion;
  - with bullet whizzes, footsteps from movement, and weapon-handling sounds at the animation's own timing;
  - with match stings and interface sounds.
- **Master limiter.** `MasterLimiter` sits on the active listener and holds the output under −1 dBFS.
- **Settings.** Effects, Interface and Ambience volume sliders.
- **Test tools.** `-audiocapture file.wav` records the final mix. Test runs log play counts and limiter figures.
  `Tools/run_relay_tests.sh combataudio` runs a live match over Relay with a host that renders and records.

**Shot results now travel with remote shots (protocol 3 → 4).** The audio test found that remote shots were being
shown wrongly. The host checks a client's shot against where that client saw its target, but every other machine
re-tested the shot against where it draws the target now. So a hit on a strafing host showed, and sounded, like a
hit on the wall behind them.

The shot event now carries the host's per-pellet result (`ShotFx.PelletHits`), and the effects follow it. The local
player gets no impact on their own body and no near-miss whizz for a bullet that hit them; the hurt sound covers both.
The message layout changed, so `BuildInfo.NetProtocol` went from 3 to 4. Builds with protocol 3 can no longer join.

The gunshot recipe was rebuilt during this work. The first version put most of its energy below 150 Hz and lost
3–8 dB through the laptop model. Wood footsteps were tonal, metal footsteps were dull, and the hum lost 10 dB. Each
was changed and measured again.

## 3. EditMode tests: 63/63 passed

The seven new tests in `AudioTests` check these things:
- the bank against the generator's manifest;
- volumes come from loudness targets, without clamping (no sound is quieter than its target);
- every sound the code asks for exists, and every sound in the bank is used;
- the import settings;
- Kappa's three machinery emitters;
- the limiter leaves audio under its ceiling bit-for-bit unchanged (after its 2 ms delay);
- the limiter holds overlapping bursts at 2.5 times full scale under the ceiling, then recovers fully.

The burst test failed on its first run: the output peaked at −0.88 dBFS, 0.12 dB over the ceiling. The gain
smoothing gets only 99.3% of the way down within the look-ahead. Each delayed frame now also carries the gain it
needs, which caps the smoothed gain as the frame leaves.

## 4. Live match over Relay

The host and client are two processes of the same build on this Mac, joined through a real Relay allocation. The
host renders and records what its listener hears. The client walks for about 10 s and then shoots the host: 33 s
with the sniper rifle and 22 s with the pistol. The host then shoots the client for about 14 s. Logs and recordings
are in `AutoTest/`, outside the repo.

| Run | Result | Sounds | Mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| `combataudio_154906` (before the limiter, the lower shot targets and the shot results) | PASS, host and client | 401 started, peak 15 voices, 0 stolen, 0 missing | **clipped**: 452 samples at full scale, loudest 400 ms at −5.7 LUFS |
| `combataudio_160007` (limiter, lower shot targets, shot results) | PASS, host and client | 342 started, peak 10 voices, 0 stolen, 0 missing | peak −1.0 dBFS, 0 samples at full scale, loudest 400 ms at −10.2 LUFS, integrated −17.4 LUFS |
| `combataudio_160351` (the whizz also follows the shot result) | PASS, host and client | 320 started, peak 11 voices, 0 stolen, 0 missing | peak −1.0 dBFS, 0 samples at full scale, loudest 400 ms at −10.2 LUFS, integrated −19.7 LUFS |

No exceptions in any of the six player logs.

**Clipping.** In the first run, all of the clipping came during the host's own rifle fire, 67–81 s into the
recording. Each shot was set to −11 LUFS, and the tails of shots 80 ms apart added up past full scale. The limiter
and 4 dB lower gunshot targets fixed it. In the second run, the limiter's input peaked at −0.5 dBFS. It reduced the
gain 1.6% of the time, by at most 0.5 dB. In the third run its input peaked at −1.0 dBFS and it reduced the gain
by 0.0 dB. It is a safety net, not part of the sound.

**Impacts.** In the first run, the host played 23 flesh impacts and 33 concrete impacts for 34 hits it took and 21
hits it made. Most of the client's hits on the host sounded like misses into the wall behind it. In the second run
the host played 21 flesh impacts, one for each of its own hits, and no concrete impacts. Hits on the host now play
only the hurt sound (28 of them, one per hit).

**Whizzes.** The second run still played 26 whizzes, although all 28 of the client's shots hit. A chest hit passes
about half a metre from the head, inside the whizz distance. The whizz now checks the host's shot result too.
In the third run, no whizzes played for the 28 bullets that hit the host.

**Distance.** The runs fought at different ranges. In the first and third, at about 20 m, every shot by the client
played both the near and the far version, and 31 and 21 weapon-handling sounds (bolts and magazines) were beyond
their 15 m range and skipped. In the second the players fought within about 9 m: no far versions, and one sound out
of range. Between them, the runs cover both sides of the crossfade.

**Panning is not shown by these runs.** The left/right correlation of the recordings is 0.99–1.00, almost mono. The
host's bot faces the client it is fighting, so the client's sounds come from straight ahead, and the host's own
sounds are non-positional. These recordings say nothing about how well sounds to the side or behind are placed.

![Recorded mix, third run](M8_audio/mix_combat.jpg)

The picture above shows the spectrogram of the third run's mix over 85 s, with the 400 ms loudness underneath.
Each band is a burst of fire. The quiet floor between bursts, around −40 to −45 LUFS, is the ambience bed and

**UI test.** The `ui` test (`ui_154418`, run before the limiter was added) records the host's whole session: menus, lobby, warmup, a short match and
the results. It played 11 sounds: countdown ×3, match start, equip, hurt, kill ×3, death and victory. None were
missing, the peak was −0.7 dBFS, and no samples reached full scale. Its host never fires, which is why the combat
audio test exists.

## 5. Known limitations

- **Synthesised, not recorded.** The sounds are original and consistent, but they do not have the detail of
  recorded and layered weapon foley.
- **One shared room tail.** Occlusion is one ray and a low-pass filter. There are no reverb zones, so indoors and
  outdoors share each sound's own baked tail.
- **No listening test.** No person has listened to the game for this report. The balance comes from loudness
  targets and from recordings the game made, not from a mix session on real speakers.
- **Low sounds on laptops.** The machinery hum (4 dB) and landings (2.6 dB) still lose level through the laptop model.
- **Shotgun results.** Hit or miss is per pellet, but which player each pellet hit is not sent. A pellet the host
  says hit, which this machine sees passing no one, is shown on the player closest to its path.
